Scholia

Free annotated editions of classic books

Classic books are free to read online, but they are full of words, places, people, and events that modern readers don’t know. Scholia puts a short note in the margin wherever a high school or college reader is likely to get stuck, and releases every note under an open license so teachers, students, and other projects can reuse them.

The name comes from the scholia (singular scholium) that readers once wrote in the margins of Greek and Latin manuscripts to explain hard words and references.

How it works

Layers you can toggle
Notes come in four layers: vocabulary, allusions, history, and places. A 10th grader can turn everything on; a college reader can hide vocabulary.
AI-drafted, human-verified
Notes are drafted with AI, then checked by a person against a cited source before they are marked verified. Until then they carry an unverified label. A missing note is better than a wrong one.
Audited
Once notes are verified, we recheck a random sample and publish the error rate.

Reading

On a wide screen the text keeps a comfortable line length in the middle of the page, and small marks beside it show where notes are. Move the pointer toward the right margin for vocabulary, or the left for allusions, history, and places. Click an underlined phrase to keep its note open.

On a phone, tap an underlined phrase and its note opens below the paragraph. In a chapter, Aa changes the theme, font, text size, and line length.

Help check notes

We need checkers. Pick a chapter, check each unverified note against a reliable source, then fix it, cut it, or mark it verified and open a pull request. Instructions are in the project’s GitHub repository.
