Scholia

Free annotated editions of the classics

Notes open under CC BY 4.0First book: Lord Jim

Classic books are free to read online, but they are full of words, places, people, and events that modern readers don’t know. Scholia sets a short note in the margin wherever a reader is likely to stop, so you can keep reading instead of searching.

The name comes from the scholia that readers once wrote in the margins of Greek and Latin manuscripts to explain hard words and references.

How the notes work

Four kinds.Notes come in four layers, each with its own printer’s mark in the margin: * vocabulary, † allusions, § history, ¶ places. Turn any layer off; a confident reader might hide vocabulary.

Checked.Notes are drafted with AI, then checked by a person against a cited source before they are marked verified. Until then they say unverified. A missing note is better than a wrong one.

Audited.Once notes are verified, we recheck a random sample and publish the error rate.

Open.Every note is released under CC BY 4.0. Teachers, students, and other projects may copy and reuse them with credit.

Reading

Hover over an underlined phrase and its note appears in the margin beside it; click to keep it there with its source. Move the pointer into either margin to see every note on that side. On a phone, tap a phrase and the note opens below the paragraph.

Help check the notes

We need checkers. Pick a chapter, check each unverified note against a reliable source, then fix it, cut it, or mark it verified and open a pull request on GitHub.
